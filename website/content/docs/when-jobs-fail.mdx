title: "When jobs fail or don't start"
description: "Find out why a job is stuck in Queued or why it failed, and what Armada does and doesn't retry."
---

This page helps you answer two questions: **why isn't my job starting?** and **why did my job fail?** It also explains what Armada retries by itself.

## Before you start

You need the job's ID (printed when you submitted it, and shown in every `watch` line and in Lookout) and its queue.

> **Placeholders:** values you fill in yourself are shown in angle brackets, like `<job-id>`. Replace the whole thing, **including the brackets**: `armada-<job-id>-0` becomes `armada-01m3z29x9mvsh3gbkhrs57a0wq-0`. If you leave the brackets in, your shell says `no such file or directory`.

## Find out what happened

Start here for any job:

| To see                              | Run                                                                | Shows                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Its history                         | `armadactl watch <queue> <job-set>`                                | Every state change, and a `Job failed:` line with the reason if it failed. Press Ctrl-C to stop.                                                                                               |
| Its result                          | Lookout: open the job, then **Result**                             | The error message, exit code and each run's node and timings.                                                                                                                                  |
| Why it isn't scheduled              | `armadactl get job-report <job-id>`                                | The scheduler's most recent decision about this job.                                                                                                                                           |
| How the scheduler treats your queue | `armadactl get queue-report <queue>`                               | What the scheduler last did for the queue, and what it holds.                                                                                                                                  |
| More detail                         | `armadactl get scheduling-report -v`                               | The scheduler's full report for the latest round. Add more `v`s (`-vvv`) for more detail. (`--job-id` still works but prints ``This flag is deprecated ... Please use `job-report` instead.``) |
| Why it failed, at a glance          | Lookout: the **Failure Category** column, or the run in **Result** | A short label such as `oom` or `user_error`, if your platform team has set up failure categories. {/* LINK: restore "[failure categories](/docs/failure-categories)" when that page merges */} |

## The job stays Queued

Run `armadactl get job-report <job-id>` and look for `UnschedulableReason`.

| `job-report` shows                                             | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                     | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `UnschedulableReason: resource limit exceeded`                 | Your queue is already using as much as its priority class allows. By default that's the whole pool, so usually your own running jobs fill it                                                      | Wait: the job starts by itself when running jobs finish. If it's urgent, see Priority and preemption. {/* LINK: restore "[Priority and preemption](/docs/priority-and-preemption)" when that page merges */}        |
| `UnschedulableReason: uniformity label <label> is not indexed` | A gang asks for a node label that isn't enabled                                                                                                                                                   | Remove the `gangNodeUniformityLabel` annotation, or ask your platform team to enable the label: the executor must report it (`kubernetes.trackedNodeLabels`) and the scheduler must index it (`indexedNodeLabels`). |
| `Most recent scheduling round that affected job <id>: none`    | The scheduler hasn't considered the job. Usually: its queue is paused (cordoned); for a gang, not all members were submitted, or its `gangId` was used before; or the job was only just submitted | Check `armadactl get queues --only-cordoned`. For a gang, see Gang jobs. {/* LINK: restore "[Gang jobs](/docs/gang-jobs#troubleshooting)" when that page merges */} Otherwise wait a minute and check again.        |

Other things to check:

- **Is the queue paused?** `armadactl get queues --only-cordoned` lists paused queues. Ask your platform team to uncordon it.
- **Is your queue getting its fair share?** `armadactl get queue-report <queue>` shows what the scheduler last allocated. If your queue is regularly under-served, its priority factor is set by your platform team.

## The job fails straight away

These jobs fail a second or two after being queued, before they ever run. The `Job failed:` line gives the reason.

| `Job failed:` says                                                                                                         | What it means                                                                                                                           | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `job/gang requests resources ... which exceeds the total limit of (memory=..., cpu=..., ...) for its queue/priority class` | The job asks for more than its queue may use in its priority class. By default that's the whole pool, so the job could never run        | Lower the job's `requests` and `limits` below the amounts in the message.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `... exceeds the total limit of (ephemeral-storage=..., nvidia.com/gpu=...)`, with no `cpu` or `memory` listed             | Armada has only just started and doesn't yet know the cluster's size                                                                    | Wait two minutes and submit again.                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `Number of nodes in cluster: 0`                                                                                            | Armada has only just started, and the executor hasn't reported its nodes yet                                                            | Wait a minute or two after installing, then submit again. Your platform team can check readiness with the scheduler's `armada_cluster_capacity{resourceType="nodes"}` metric (above 0 when ready) or its log line `Retrieved 1 executors`. |
| `Number of nodes in cluster: N` ... `N: insufficient resources available`                                                  | No node can fit the job. The lines above it say why each node was ruled out. A job bigger than the whole cluster can also fail this way | Lower the job's `requests` and `limits`. A `taint ... not tolerated` line for a control-plane node is normal.                                                                                                                              |
| `cannot submit more jobs to gang than specified in gang cardinality`                                                       | A gang has more members than its `gangCardinality`                                                                                      | See Gang jobs. {/* LINK: restore "[Gang jobs](/docs/gang-jobs#troubleshooting)" when that page merges */}                                                                                                                                  |

Errors that appear when you **submit** (such as `container <name> has no resources specified`) are covered in Submitting jobs. {/* LINK: restore "[Submitting jobs](/docs/submitting-jobs#troubleshooting)" when that page merges */}

## The job fails while running

| `Job failed:` says                                                                                                              | What it means                                                                                                       | What to do                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Container <name> failed with exit code <code> because Error: `                                                                 | Your program exited with an error                                                                                   | Read its output while the pod still exists: `kubectl logs -n <namespace> armada-<job-id>-0` on a laptop, or the Lookout **Logs** tab on a team installation. Fix and resubmit.                        |
| `Container <name> failed with exit code 137 because OOMKilled: `                                                                | The container used more memory than its limit                                                                       | Raise the memory `requests` and `limits`, or use less memory. A retry policy {/* LINK: restore "[retry policy](/docs/retry-policies)" when that page merges */} can add memory automatically.         |
| `Unable to start pod - encountered an unrecoverable problem.` followed by `Matched check: ...` and `Failed to pull image ...`   | The image doesn't exist or can't be pulled, so the pod never started                                                | Check the image name and tag. This takes about 1.5 to 3 minutes to fail: Armada waits 90 seconds after the pod's last change in case the pull recovers.                                               |
| `Pod was active on the node longer than the specified deadline`                                                                 | The job ran past its `activeDeadlineSeconds`                                                                        | Raise the deadline, or make the job faster.                                                                                                                                                           |
| `Retry policy: ...` (for example `policy retry limit exceeded (2/2)`), or `Lease expired and not retried by policy: ...`        | Armada retried the job under a retry policy and has stopped, or the policy didn't retry a run lost with its cluster | See Retry policies. {/* LINK: restore "[Retry policies](/docs/retry-policies#troubleshooting)" when that page merges */} The earlier runs in Lookout's **Result** tab show the program's own errors.  |
| `Job was attempted <n> times, and has been tried once on all nodes it can run on` or `Maximum number of attempts (<n>) reached` | Armada retried a job that couldn't start, and has given up                                                          | Look at the earlier runs in Lookout for the cause. If retry policies are on, see Retry policies. {/* LINK: restore "[Retry policies](/docs/retry-policies#troubleshooting)" when that page merges */} |
| `Preempted by ...`                                                                                                              | Armada or a person stopped the job on purpose                                                                       | See Priority and preemption. {/* LINK: restore "[Priority and preemption](/docs/priority-and-preemption)" when that page merges */}                                                                   |
| Reason `Evicted`                                                                                                                | Kubernetes stopped the pod, usually because its node ran short of memory or disk                                    | Resubmit. If it keeps happening on the same nodes, tell your platform team.                                                                                                                           |

Read the output soon. Finished pods are cleaned up after a few minutes (failed ones after about ten), and their logs go with them; with some retry setups the pod is deleted straight away. Lookout keeps the job's state, error message and exit code.

`armadactl watch` may suggest `kubectl --context Cluster1 ... logs ...` for a failed job. On a laptop install, use `--context kind-armada` (or leave `--context` out) instead.

## What Armada retries, and what it doesn't

| What happened                                                                                                               | Does Armada retry?                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The pod couldn't start for a reason that might pass (for example, it can't be placed on the node, or an image pull is slow) | **Yes**, by itself: it puts the job back in the queue, possibly for another node. These are **startup retries**. To fail straight away instead, set the annotation `armadaproject.io/failFast: "true"`. Gang jobs always fail straight away. |
| The image doesn't exist                                                                                                     | **No.** It fails after 1.5 to 3 minutes.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Your program exited with an error, or ran out of memory                                                                     | **Only if a retry policy {/* LINK: restore "[retry policy](/docs/retry-policies)" when that page merges */} says so.** Without one, the job ends as failed.                                                                                  |
| The job was preempted                                                                                                       | **No.** The job ends as failed.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

### Automatic retries with retry policies

Your platform team can turn on retry policies {/* LINK: restore "[retry policies](/docs/retry-policies)" when that page merges */} and attach them to queues. A policy uses each failure's category {/* LINK: restore "[category](/docs/failure-categories)" when that page merges */} to decide: for example, retry `oom` with 50% more memory, but never retry `user_error`. A retried job keeps its job ID and goes back to the queue; Lookout's **Result** tab lists each run. They're off by default: ask your platform team whether your queue has one (`armadactl get queue <queue>` lists them under `retryPolicies`).

If you use events in your own tools, a failed event with `retryable: true` isn't the end of the job. See Retry policies. {/* LINK: restore "[Retry policies](/docs/retry-policies#dont-treat-every-failed-event-as-final)" when that page merges */}

### Resubmitting yourself

When a job fails and the work still matters, you resubmit it. To make that safe:

- **Use a workflow tool's retries.** With Airflow, {/* LINK: restore "[Airflow](/docs/airflow)" when that page merges */} the task's `retries` resubmit a failed job.
- **Use `clientId`** if your own code resubmits, so a repeated submission doesn't create a duplicate job (see Submitting jobs). {/* LINK: restore "[Submitting jobs](/docs/submitting-jobs#job-file-fields)" when that page merges */}
- **Make the job safe to run twice**, for example by writing outputs to a new location each time.

## Troubleshooting

| What you see                                                                                          | Cause                                                               | Fix                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A job stays **Pending** for minutes                                                                   | The pod is waiting for its image, or for something else on the node | Wait: Armada fails or retries it after its checks run out (90 seconds for a missing image, up to 15 minutes for a slow pull). `kubectl get events -n <namespace>` shows what the pod is waiting for. |
| `Found no logs for job; try 'kubectl --context Cluster1 ...`                                          | `watch` uses the executor's name as the kubectl context             | On a laptop, use `--context kind-armada`.                                                                                                                                                            |
| ``This flag is deprecated and will be removed in a future release. Please use `job-report` instead.`` | You used `scheduling-report --job-id`                               | Use `armadactl get job-report <job-id>`.                                                                                                                                                             |
| `zsh: no such file or directory: job-id` (or `bash: job-id: No such file or directory`)               | A placeholder was pasted with its angle brackets                    | Replace `<job-id>`, including `<` and `>`, with the real value.                                                                                                                                      |
